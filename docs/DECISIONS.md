# Sea design decisions

A record of why Sea is built the way it is. Entries up to SEA-D15 are reconstructed (16 October 2026) from the code
comments, the README, `engine-notes.md` and the version notes; their "introduced" versions are approximate. Entries
from SEA-D16 record decisions made on that date or later. Suite-level direction lives in
[overview/DECISIONS.md](../../overview/DECISIONS.md); this file covers this mod only.

Status values: **Active** (implemented and current), **Superseded**, **Proposal** (not implemented, needs agreement).

| SEA-D01 | 0.2 | The wind is a **pure function** of (wind key, world shape, config, position, game time). | SEA-D05 | 0.2 | **Latitude comes from the game calendar** (`OnGetLatitude`), read live. | SEA-D06 | 0.3 | The waves are a **fixed library of wave trains** (20 swell wavelengths × 18 directions + 3 chop since pre.8; formerly 12 + 2) made from the wind key. The sea state only sets their **amplitudes**; no train ever changes its phase | Ships need the surface on the CPU, the same on the server and every client, with nothing jumping when the weather changes. A per-frame noise or an FFT ocean can't be sampled cheaply and identically on every side | At most 24 trains are drawn at once (uniform budget, SEA-D11). On a Cylinder World ring every train fits a whole number of waves around it |
| SEA-D07 | 0.3 | The **wave clock is the server's running time** (`ElapsedMilliseconds`), sent every 5 s plus half the ping. Clients slew at most 2 %, and snap only when more than 1 s off | The calendar jumps on every server update and runs fast while players sleep | Waves never jump on a client. A paused singleplayer game stops the clock, as the server's does |
| SEA-D08 | 0.1 | **Sea owns the water shaders.** Its code is added as each shader loads, using text anchors. Ship mods register clip sources instead of patching the shaders. | SEA-D09 | 0.3 | The **sheltered-water factor** (lakes get a fifth) is read from the region's `OceanMap` exactly as the chunk tesselator packs it into the water vertices | The server's ship motion then matches the waves drawn there | It follows whatever the world's `OceanMap` says, not the actual coastline (see [compatibility.md](compatibility.md)) |
| SEA-D10 | 0.3.0 | **Dry hulls are cut per pixel** in the water fragment shaders, and dryness for the camera, breathing, splashes and particles is answered by the ship mod (`IDrySpaces`) | A ship is an entity, not blocks, so the world's water can't be removed from inside a moving hull | Each underwater effect needs its own patch (camera, breathing, splashes, particles). | SEA-D15 | 0.3 | **Sea state follows the past 16 game hours of wind** at the same place: a fast build-up and a slower swell decay. | SEA-D16 | 16 Oct 2026 (0.3.6) | **Both ends of each second's amplitude blend are worked out from fixed moments**, the game hours of samples n − 1 and n, at the place's current position. That broke the "same sea for everyone" contract (Codex review, 16 Oct) | `SeaAt` and `WindAt` are now worked out twice per place and second (~0.1 ms). The fields vary over ≥ 300 blocks, so a grid would add steps without making ships agree better. | SEA-D17 | 16 Oct 2026 (0.3.6) | **The dry cut uses the hull's exact frame**, rebuilt from its waterline slopes: pitch = atan(`SlopeAlong`), tan(roll) = −`SlopeAcross`·cos(pitch), roll about the keel applied before pitch, measured from the waterline point above (X, Z). No new API fields | The small-angle shear used before left strips of water inside a hull that was pitched and rolled together. The keel staying in the vertical plane of the heading fixes the frame uniquely from the two slopes, matching Hansa's `ShipFrame` | Exact for a hull whose origin is on its waterline. One whose origin is h below it is off by about h·tan(tilt) (Hansa: h = 0.1). The C# twin (`WaterClipBox.HullFrame`) and the GPU check agree pixel for pixel. Possibly related to the stern dry-outline failure in the ship test history; not verified |
| SEA-D20 | 16 Oct 2026 (0.3.6) | **A sample's game hour comes from the server's anchors** (`SampleHours`). Every 5 s the server says: from sample n + 3 on, hours run from the calendar's present reading at its present speed. It also checks once a second, and makes a new anchor at once when the calendar has left the newest one's line by more than 3 game minutes (`/time set`, sleep starting or ending). Each clock packet carries every anchor the server keeps, numbered in the order made; each side drops one once a later one covers every sample from two back on (so a burst of corrections can't drop one still needed), and ignores one it already had or dropped. Each side uses, for each sample, the latest anchor in force there. Until a client hears of one, it makes its own from its calendar | The calendar can't give a sample's hour. The server's moves in 200 ms steps (`ServerSystemCalendar`), each client's is a little off, and sleeping runs it ~20× faster. So "now worked back to the sample's start" differed between callers in the same second, and between sides (Codex second pass). A client joining on an anchor's first sample needs the one before it for its first blend (Codex third pass) | Every caller on every side gets the same hours for both ends of a sample's blend, once the client has the anchors (checked offline with a stepped, sleeping, jumping calendar and late clients). The sample hours lag a sudden calendar change by up to ~5 s of it. When sleep starts that's about 20 game minutes, which only affects the gusts. A client that hears of an anchor after it's in force (more than ~3 s of lag) differs until it does. The clock packet's anchors are a new optional field |
